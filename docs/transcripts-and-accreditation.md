# Transcripts, credits, and accreditation

This document explains exactly what the records in Texas Home Academy mean and
what they do not mean. It is written to be read alongside
`src/content/frameworks.ts` and `src/content/sources.ts`.

## What Texas law says

- A child subject to compulsory attendance may be exempt if the child "attends a
  private or parochial school that includes in its course a study of good
  citizenship" (Tex. Educ. Code §25.086(a)(1)).
- The Texas Supreme Court held that a bona fide home school qualifies for that
  exemption: *Texas Education Agency v. Leeper*, 893 S.W.2d 432 (Tex. 1994). The
  curriculum must be conducted in good faith and designed to meet basic
  education goals of reading, spelling, grammar, mathematics, and a study of
  good citizenship.
- TEA states that it does not regulate, index, monitor, approve, register, or
  accredit home schools, and that the State of Texas does not award a diploma to
  home-schooled students.

Texas does not require a home school to keep records. Records are kept here
because colleges, scholarship programs, the NCAA, and employers may ask for
them, and because a family cannot reconstruct four years of coursework later.

## What the transcript is

The transcript is a **parent-issued home-school record**. It reports:

- course titles and the credit value assigned to each;
- semester and final numeric grades computed from recorded assessments;
- letter grades and quality points;
- credit-weighted unweighted and weighted GPA;
- completed credits and the status of the Foundation High School Program credit
  pattern.

It is not a state document, not a TEA record, and not an accredited school's
record.

## Credit conventions

- A full-year course (two semesters) earns 1 credit.
- A one-semester course earns 0.5 credit.
- Credit is awarded only when the final grade is 70 or higher.
- A final grade is issued only after the cumulative final examination is
  recorded.

These are this program's documented conventions. They follow the way Texas
schools record credit and the minimum passing standard in 19 TAC §74.26(c). They
are not a claim that any other institution must accept the credit.

## GPA

- Quality points: A 4.0, B 3.0, C 2.0, D 1.0, F 0.0.
- Advanced courses add 0.5, capped at 4.5.
- GPA is credit-weighted and includes only credit-bearing high-school courses
  with an issued final grade. K–8 coursework never enters the GPA.
- The unweighted GPA is reported on a 4.0 scale without the advanced bonus.

## Graduation audit

The audit compares recorded credits against the published Foundation High School
Program: 22 credits for the foundation plan and 26 for the endorsement plan,
with the five endorsement areas and the Distinguished Level of Achievement
(19 TAC §§74.11–74.13; TEA Graduation Toolkit 2025).

Passing the audit means the student's recorded credits match the published
pattern. It does **not**:

- confer a Texas diploma;
- make this program accredited;
- guarantee admission to any college or university;
- guarantee transfer of credit;
- establish NCAA eligibility.

NCAA initial-eligibility review of home-school core courses is a separate process
with its own documentation requirements, including an administrator statement and
core-course worksheets. Texas public colleges set their own admission and
transcript procedures, and the Texas Success Initiative applies to entering,
non-exempt students at Texas public institutions.

## Attendance

Texas public schools require at least 90% attendance to earn class credit
(Tex. Educ. Code §25.092). This program adopts the same 90% standard as its own
promotion and credit policy. A home school is not bound by that statute; the
standard is adopted deliberately so records are defensible and comparable.

The school-year calendar is generated from the fourth Monday in August
(Tex. Educ. Code §25.0811) and 180 instructional days (§25.081). These statutes
govern school districts, not private home schools; they are adopted here as
program policy.

## What families should keep

- course descriptions and reading lists;
- samples of graded work across the year;
- assessment records and rubrics;
- attendance logs;
- any standardized test scores, if the family chooses to test.

Export a JSON backup from Settings regularly. Browser storage can be cleared, and
this app has no cloud copy of anything.
