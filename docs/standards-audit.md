# Standards and compliance audit

Last reviewed: 2026-11-16. Every source listed here was accessed on that date.

This document records what each course in Texas Home Academy claims to teach, which Texas
Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS) chapter it maps to, which government source supports the
claim, and where the curriculum does **not** yet meet the standard. It is deliberately written to
be uncomfortable to read: a gap that is listed here can be fixed, a gap that is hidden cannot.

## 1. What this program is and is not

| Claim | Status | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This is a home school operated by a family, not a school district or charter. | True | Tex. Educ. Code §25.086(a)(1); *TEA v. Leeper*, 893 S.W.2d 432 (Tex. 1994) |
| TEA approves, accredits, indexes, or monitors this curriculum. | **False.** TEA does none of these for home schools. | TEA, *Home Schooling* |
| This program issues a State of Texas diploma. | **False.** The State of Texas does not award diplomas to home-schooled students. | TEA, *Home Schooling* |
| Records here can be used for college admission, scholarships, and employment. | True, as home-school records, with the documentation each institution requests. | THSC record-keeping guidance; Texas A&M home-school admissions guidance; NCAA home-school eligibility rules |
| The transcript is an official school transcript in the TEA sense. | **False.** It is a parent-issued home-school record. | TEA, *Home Schooling*; see `docs/transcripts-and-accreditation.md` |
| Credits follow the state credit definition (70/100 minimum). | Adopted voluntarily as a grading floor. | 19 TAC §74.26(c) |

Nothing in this repository has been reviewed or endorsed by TEA, SBOE, or any accrediting body.
No claim of accreditation is made anywhere in the application or documentation.

## 2. Required curriculum coverage

Texas requires a bona fide home school to teach reading, spelling, grammar, mathematics, and good
citizenship (*Leeper*). 19 TAC Chapter 74 Subchapter A sets the required curriculum for public
schools; it does not bind home schools, but this program follows it as its design standard.

**Assessment requirements.** STAAR and the five end-of-course assessments apply to students
enrolled in Texas public and open-enrollment charter schools (TEA, *STAAR Resources*). A home
school is not a public school, so these tests are not a graduation condition here. Families whose
students plan to attend a Texas public college should expect the Texas Success Initiative
assessment or an exemption (THCB, *Texas Success Initiative*), and student-athletes should expect
the NCAA Eligibility Center's separate home-school review (NCAA, *Homeschool Student Initial
Eligibility Requirements*). Neither is administered by this program.

**Endorsements.** The five endorsement areas are summarized from the TEA Graduation Toolkit 2025.
The in-app audit is a planning aid: the statutory conditions live in 19 TAC §74.13, and a home
school parent, not this software, certifies completion.

## 5. Grading and assessment policy

Applied to every course and printed on each course page:

| Component | Weight | Grading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practice and daily work | per course weights | Auto-graded, best attempt recorded |
| Unit quizzes | per course weights | Auto-graded objective items; written items scored against a published rubric |
| Projects and writing | per course weights | Parent-scored against a 100-point analytic rubric |
| Semester 1 | 80% coursework (Q1–Q3) + 20% midterm | Computed |
| Semester 2 | 80% coursework (Q4–Q6) + 20% cumulative final | Computed |
| Course final grade | Average of the two semester grades | Issued only after the final examination record exists |

Retakes are unlimited for practice and capped at two scored attempts for quizzes and exams, with
the highest score counting. Grades below 70 require remediation before credit is awarded; the
gradebook and graduation audit both enforce the 70 floor. No lesson completion, page view, or
"mark as read" action produces a grade.

## 6. Gap register

1. **Lesson depth — met, measured honestly.** The self-teaching standard (v2) asks for
   four objectives, three sections, grade-scaled taught prose (350 words K–2, 400 in 3–5, 500 in
   6–8, 600 in 9–12), three worked examples with reasoning and answers, eight defined terms, five
   practice items, four off-screen exercises, four reviewed questions with model answers, three
   discussed topics, and a real-world application section, and it rejects shared boilerplate.
   All two hundred one K–12 lessons meet v2, so `src/content/depth/shortfalls.ts` is empty and the
   Sources page reports 201 of 201 at standard. `src/content/depth.test.ts` enforces v2 and fails
   with the lesson name and the missed criterion, and its ledger check is a bijection: every short
   lesson must be listed with its measured counts and every listed lesson must still be short, so
   the ledger cannot silently drift in either direction. Nothing was padded to reach this point; the
   remaining lessons were authored, and the standard, thresholds and checks were left unchanged.
   Meeting this internal depth standard is not a compliance or accreditation claim: TEKS alignment,
   graduation-credit handling and the limits of homeschool records are separate matters, catalogued
   elsewhere in this register.
2. **Written-response scoring.** Written items are scored by a parent against a rubric. The app
   does not evaluate prose and does not claim to.
3. **Practical/performance assessments.** PE activity logs, studio art production, and lab work
   are documented by the family. The app records the project grade, not the activity itself.
4. **LOTE sequence.** Only Spanish I and II exist; a third level is needed for the three-credit
   LOTE endorsement path.
5. **CTE programs of study.** Two single CTE courses exist, not complete programs of study.
6. **Advanced math/science breadth.** Statistics and Environmental Science appear in the
   graduation audit but are not yet full courses.
7. **Accommodations.** The app supports untimed attempts and reading at any pace, but there is no
   formal IEP/504 record feature; IDEA and Section 504 apply to public schools, not home schools.
